# Experimental monster gauges (first implementation slice)

**Status:** draft, opt-in. This is *not* a proven host-authoritative reading and must not be enabled by default until verified against a running supported build. Phase tracking: #14. Attribution research: #15.

## Scope

The plugin adds a protected, read-only, bounded `MonsterGaugeReader` for **confirmed MHW build 421810 only**, gated by `experimentalMonsterGauges` in `settings.json` (F9 checkbox). It refreshes on an independent **1.0 s** cadence (separate from the 100 ms DPS poll), scheduled on a monotonic process clock rather than the quest timer. Recording intervals use that same monotonic clock. When the game's quest timer changes source or jumps, stored sample `t` stays in nondecreasing capture order; `rawHuntT` retains the original observed quest time, `clockAdjusted` marks the persistent normalization offset, while optional `clockBoundary` identifies **only the next recorded sample per monster after a real clock discontinuity**. The producer latches this marker across the two-second throttle; subsequent recordings explicitly carry `clockBoundary: false`. For older schema-3 files lacking this field, viewers conservatively infer the first `clockAdjusted` false→true edge. Top-level `gaugeClockDiscontinuities` counts timeline changes for both viewers. **Across a discontinuity, gauge `t` and older HP-hit/party-event timestamps may not align**; neither viewer should imply precise cross-metric attribution. Unreadable/torn monster enumerations immediately clear the experimental overlay state. A changed type/variant/name or a large **proportional** HP reset at the same native pointer invalidates cached gauges and forces an immediate refresh. Max-HP changes **alone** are not considered replacement: Iceborne dynamically rescales monster HP when hunters join/leave. The HP-only damage fallback preserves its previously counted total across rescaling and rebases future raw damage observations to avoid phantom damage or duplicate monster IDs. Local per-hit records bind their fight-local monster ID and type synchronously at hook time, before polling can retire/reassign the native pointer; buffered hits are never reassigned from m1 to m2 on drain. A hit captured before any known monster mapping remains unattributed instead of being guessed after a spawn. An excluded/small or positively readable **strictly positive** low-max-HP entity reusing a large-monster address is treated as confirmed untracked and retires the former large-monster fallback/recorder mapping; truly unreadable type/HP (including zero or negative max HP) retains it until enumeration can establish replacement. Ailment pointer-list terminators must be exactly 0 or 1; other invalid signed pointers are skipped without hiding later slots. A hit arriving in the same polling interval as a rescale cannot be reliably distinguished from the health-scale change and is conservatively not inferred; genuine generation changes still bank earlier monster damage even below the older 25% reset threshold; party-read exceptions do not suppress healthy monster observations. Independently invalid ailment entries are skipped and a `0` or `1` terminator ends the scan, consistent with HunterPie's pointer walk. An unsupported/unconfirmed build never polls these extra structures. Missing/invalid readings remain absent; an unavailable meter is not treated as zero.

- Monster stamina: current and maximum at entity `+0x1C0F0`.
- Normal part flinch/break meters: monster part-controller pointer at `+0x1D058`, normal array `+0x40 + slot*0x1F8`; max, remaining, counter. Collision ordinal resolves to the canonical part id in `monster-parts.json`, skipping severable definitions. Next break threshold uses HunterPie metadata.
- Ailments: bounded pointer list at monster `+0x1BC40`, struct at ailment pointer `+0x148`, validating owner, id, buildup, max buildup, current/maximum duration, proc count. Includes KO/stun and exhaust buildup.
- Alatreon's exceptional elemental-explosion counter (monster 87, canonical part ID 3) is intentionally omitted rather than mislabeled as an ordinary flinch cycle; verifying a separate interpretation is tracked under #17/#18.
- The in-game overlay shows only a small preview of active ailments and the next-break part gauges. All readings are visibly labelled experimental/non-authoritative.
- When opt-in is on, the recorder writes at most one sample every two seconds per monster into optional schema-3 `gaugeSamples` (max 3600 hunt-wide, with `gaugeSamplesDropped` recording omitted observation intervals if capped), with source `local-unverified`. Both viewer parsers accept this field, defaulting to an empty array for old logs. The stacked viewer follow-up (#36) now implements **sampled gauge-history charts**; precise proc events, verified attribution and event timelines remain separate work.

## Historical viewing (issue #19 — stacked follow-up)

A separate, draft viewer-only follow-up against #16 adds a first read-only Monster State History panel in **both** SolidJS and GPUI viewers. The user can select a recorded fight-local monster (`m1`, `m2`, etc.) and an observed metric: stamina remaining, ailment/KO/Exhaust buildup, or normal-part flinch remaining, expressed as a **percentage of the sampled maximum**. These are different measurements and must not be totaled with HP damage. The panel shows `local-unverified` provenance; no host authority, per-hunter part/status contribution, or exact break/proc time is inferred.

A missing/invalid gauge remains missing, not zero; actual per-monster clock boundaries, observation-source changes and intervals longer than six seconds create independent chart segments instead of falsely implying intervening values. A change of source does not establish either source's authority: all experimental observations remain unverified until the runtime gate in #17. A persistent clock normalization offset alone must not split every subsequent sample. Capture order is preserved. Both viewers use a fixed 0–100% Y-axis for comparable percentages, include isolated readings in the time domain, and display up to five isolated observations explicitly rather than joining missing intervals. Older logs with no `gaugeSamples` show an explicit empty state. Imported logs with malformed optional gauge entries retain their normal hunt, hit, and DPS history: unrecognizable gauge samples and nested elements are skipped, while a recognizable monster observation with an invalid timestamp is retained only as a non-plottable gap marker (`t=-1`) so neither viewer connects across a known missing/clock-corrected interval. Current scope is trend visualization only; future inferred/verified event timelines, tenderize/enrage/sever gauges, and deeper coverage/authority metadata remain separate in #18/#19 and live validation #17. This viewer code can be reviewed and tested without running MHW, but must not be taken as runtime gauge verification.

## Stored-sample quality reporting (issue #19; next stacked slice)

Both SolidJS and GPUI viewers report **counts of stored observations per selected monster and metric**, not estimated gameplay coverage. Observation-source changes are counted in stored capture order, even when a source switch occurs on an invalid or out-of-order timestamp; only time-gap lengths require comparable timestamps. The per-monster summary remains visible when all retained samples are non-plottable and no metric is selectable: it reports stored observations, unreadable timestamps, observed inter-capture gaps longer than six seconds, source changes, recorded clock-boundary flags (including the legacy `clockAdjusted` transition fallback), and non-default source labels. Only when a numeric metric is selected do the viewers additionally report usable samples, missing/invalid metric values, and metric-specific out-of-order readings. Non-default source labels are counted but **never promoted into verified host authority**. Invalid timestamps can be retained as non-plottable gap markers; some completely malformed records were skipped during import and cannot be counted. A zero count is therefore **not** proof that no in-game failure occurred.

Top-level `gaugeSamplesDropped` is a **hunt-wide** count of observation intervals omitted at the recording cap, not a per-monster or per-metric count, and must not be allocated to a selected monster. `gaugeClockDiscontinuities` is likewise hunt-wide. Both viewers show these counts independently of monster and metric availability, including when all optional gauge records are rejected during import. There is no reliable denominator for expected in-game observation coverage in schema 3; the UI deliberately shows **no percent coverage, inferred exact event time, or claim of verified reading quality**. Reported gaps require consecutive comparable valid-time *stored* samples for a monster: the comparison resets at a real or inferred clock boundary, an invalid timestamp, or a backward timestamp (which cannot become the next baseline). Source changes are still counted separately in capture order. These counts cannot detect unrecorded failures without additional evidence.

## Observed counter-change intervals (next viewer slice, issue #19)

Both viewers can list increases in **recorded** ailment proc counters and normal-part completed-flinch counters, alongside the selected percentage history. Each entry means only that the counter increased **between two consecutive comparable local samples** (shown as a start/end interval), not that a specific proc, break, hit or attacker occurred at either endpoint. A single interval can contain several increments.

Comparisons reset at missing/invalid observations, clock discontinuities, changes of observation source, non-monotonic or >6-second gaps, missing counters and counter decreases. No event is synthesized across such gaps and no counter is inferred as zero merely because an imported optional field is absent. This is retrospective analysis of `local-unverified` samples, not new memory access, an authoritative in-game event feed or synchronized HP/DPS attribution. Exact proc/break events, cross-client source authority and gameplay validation remain deferred under #17/#18.

## Explicitly deferred

- **Still experimental:** monster-generation inference and cumulative HP-damage banking from identity/large HP recovery are conservative. Difficulty scaling without a stable hit-by-hit source can hide a hit coincident with the exact rebase tick; a complete monster enumeration that observes a pointer disappearing clears only its *live* monster reference; the historical mN is retained and any same-pointer reappearance receives a new mN even with identical type/name/variant/HP. Torn/failed enumerations never count as disappearance, and successful enumeration with an individually unreadable monster HP/type retains that pointer using the tracker's known live-instance list. Pointer reuse that occurs **without an observed absence or identity/HP signal** may remain ambiguous until runtime testing. The normalized gauge clock is explicitly separate from raw hit/quest timestamps after a discontinuity.
- **Live verification remains required:** `0`/`1`-terminated ailment-list packing, structure owners and offsets, native-copy overhead under Windows/Proton and host/guest authority are still unverified.
- Separate severable part memory region (`+0x1FC8`), tenderization-duration array (`+0x1C458`), enrage state, exact proc timing, per-hit buildup, host/guest authority detection, polished widgets, and **verified proc/break event charts and event timelines**. Both viewers now provide **read-only sampled gauge-history graphs** in stacked PR #36; neither viewer provides precise proc/break timing or verified combat-event attribution.
- Current collision hit damage continues to mean **HP damage by contact part**; it is not part buildup, KO, or ailment damage.
- No game-memory writes, remote service, or new detours.

## Research basis

Offsets follow the [HunterPie MHWMonster reader](https://github.com/HunterPie/HunterPie/blob/main/HunterPie.Integrations/Datasources/MonsterHunterWorld/Entity/Enemy/MHWMonster.cs) and its [part](https://github.com/HunterPie/HunterPie/blob/main/HunterPie.Integrations/Datasources/MonsterHunterWorld/Entity/Enemy/MHWMonsterPart.cs) and [ailment](https://github.com/HunterPie/HunterPie/blob/main/HunterPie.Integrations/Datasources/MonsterHunterWorld/Entity/Enemy/MHWMonsterAilment.cs) structures. `data/monster-parts.json` was enriched from HunterPie's Apache-2.0 World MonsterData.xml. These are **reference layouts**, not evidence they work in this binary without verification.

## Live verification required

1. On 421810, load a solo Anjanath and compare stamina, head/leg flinch gauges and KO status with HunterPie. Ensure data is read while entering/leaving quest and on a reused monster pointer.
2. Use a Hammer for KO, elemental-status weapon for paralysis/poison, part break with/without Partbreaker; watch actual gauge resets and counters.
3. Compare solo, multiplayer host and multiplayer guest. Test transient zero/negative max-HP observations and corrupt negative middle ailment pointers without false pointer retirement or lost later ailments. Verify pending hit attribution over disappear/respawn at reused pointers and excluded/small monster reuse without stale hook targets. Test a mid-fight 1→2→1-player join/leave while tracking raw/max HP, the accumulated HP-fallback damage, fight-local IDs and gauge caching; confirm no false monster generation or inferred damage on pure HP rescale. Do not treat guest values as authoritative without independent confirmation.
4. Run under Windows and Proton, with and without the feature enabled. Confirm the plugin still loads and games never crash during rapid despawns or quest load.
5. After these pass, proceed to #14 log capture/viewers and full gauge widget; keep #15 distinct.

Run `dotnet run --project tests/PluginRegression` for simulated-reading regressions, **not** a substitute for in-game validation.
