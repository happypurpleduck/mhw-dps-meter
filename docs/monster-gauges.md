# Experimental monster gauges (first implementation slice)

**Status:** draft, opt-in. This is *not* a proven host-authoritative reading and must not be enabled by default until verified against a running supported build. Phase tracking: #14. Attribution research: #15.

## Scope

The plugin adds a protected, read-only, bounded `MonsterGaugeReader` for **confirmed MHW build 421810 only**, gated by `experimentalMonsterGauges` in `settings.json` (F9 checkbox). It refreshes on an independent **1.0 s** cadence (separate from the 100 ms DPS poll), scheduled on a monotonic process clock rather than the quest timer. Recording intervals use that same monotonic clock. When the game's quest timer changes source or jumps, stored sample `t` stays in nondecreasing capture order; `rawHuntT` retains the original observed quest time, `clockAdjusted` marks the persistent normalization offset, while optional `clockBoundary` identifies **only the next recorded sample per monster after a real clock discontinuity**. The producer latches this marker across the two-second throttle; subsequent recordings explicitly carry `clockBoundary: false`. For older schema-3 files lacking this field, viewers conservatively infer the first `clockAdjusted` false→true edge. Top-level `gaugeClockDiscontinuities` counts timeline changes for both viewers. **Across a discontinuity, gauge `t` and older HP-hit/party-event timestamps may not align**; neither viewer should imply precise cross-metric attribution. Unreadable/torn monster enumerations immediately clear the experimental overlay state. A changed type/variant/name or a large **proportional** HP reset at the same native pointer invalidates cached gauges and forces an immediate refresh. Max-HP changes **alone** are not considered replacement: Iceborne dynamically rescales monster HP when hunters join/leave. The HP-only damage fallback preserves its previously counted total across rescaling and rebases future raw damage observations to avoid phantom damage or duplicate monster IDs. Local per-hit records bind their fight-local monster ID and type synchronously at hook time, before polling can retire/reassign the native pointer; buffered hits are never reassigned from m1 to m2 on drain. A hit captured before any known monster mapping remains unattributed instead of being guessed after a spawn. An excluded/small or positively readable **strictly positive** low-max-HP entity reusing a large-monster address is treated as confirmed untracked and retires the former large-monster fallback/recorder mapping; truly unreadable type/HP (including zero or negative max HP) retains it until enumeration can establish replacement. Ailment pointer-list terminators must be exactly 0 or 1; other invalid signed pointers are skipped without hiding later slots. A hit arriving in the same polling interval as a rescale cannot be reliably distinguished from the health-scale change and is conservatively not inferred; genuine generation changes still bank earlier monster damage even below the older 25% reset threshold; party-read exceptions do not suppress healthy monster observations. Independently invalid ailment entries are skipped and a `0` or `1` terminator ends the scan, consistent with HunterPie's pointer walk. An unsupported/unconfirmed build never polls these extra structures. Missing/invalid readings remain absent; an unavailable meter is not treated as zero.

- Monster stamina: current and maximum at entity `+0x1C0F0`.
- Normal part flinch/break meters: monster part-controller pointer at `+0x1D058`, normal array `+0x40 + slot*0x1F8`; max, remaining, counter. Collision ordinal resolves to the canonical part id in `monster-parts.json`, skipping severable definitions. Next break threshold uses HunterPie metadata.
- Ailments: bounded pointer list at monster `+0x1BC40`, struct at ailment pointer `+0x148`, validating owner, id, buildup, max buildup, current/maximum duration, proc count. Includes KO/stun and exhaust buildup.
- Alatreon's exceptional elemental-explosion counter (monster 87, canonical part ID 3) is intentionally omitted rather than mislabeled as an ordinary flinch cycle; verifying a separate interpretation is tracked under #17/#18.
- The in-game overlay shows only a small preview of active ailments and the next-break part gauges. All readings are visibly labelled experimental/non-authoritative.
- When opt-in is on, the recorder writes at most one sample every two seconds per monster into optional schema-3 `gaugeSamples` (max 3600 hunt-wide, with `gaugeSamplesDropped` recording omitted observation intervals if capped), with source `local-unverified`. Both viewer parsers accept this field, defaulting to an empty array for old logs. The stacked viewer follow-up (#36) now implements **sampled gauge-history charts**; precise proc events, verified attribution and event timelines remain separate work.

## Historical viewing (issue #19 — stacked follow-up)

A separate, draft viewer-only follow-up against #16 adds a first read-only Monster State History panel in **both** SolidJS and GPUI viewers. The user can select a recorded fight-local monster (`m1`, `m2`, etc.) and an observed metric: stamina remaining, ailment/KO/Exhaust buildup, independently sampled ailment-duration remaining, or normal-part flinch remaining, expressed as a **percentage of the sampled maximum**. A duration percentage is a fraction of the sampled maximum remaining time, **not an inferred exact status activation/expiration event**, and it is never added to buildup or HP. These are different measurements and must not be totaled with HP damage. The panel shows `local-unverified` provenance; no host authority, per-hunter part/status contribution, or exact break/proc time is inferred.

A duration metric is only selectable when the imported observation actually has a valid duration and positive maximum; duplicated partial records for the same ailment ID are checked per metric and the first valid current/maximum pair within a single record is used (never mixed across entries); partial logs with duration-only data are supported in both viewers, while absent/invalid duration remains unknown rather than a fabricated zero. A zero with a present, positive maximum is a valid sampled reading. A missing/invalid gauge remains missing, not zero; actual per-monster clock boundaries, observation-source changes and intervals longer than six seconds create independent chart segments instead of falsely implying intervening values. A change of source does not establish either source's authority: all experimental observations remain unverified until the runtime gate in #17. A persistent clock normalization offset alone must not split every subsequent sample. Capture order is preserved. Both viewers use a fixed 0–100% Y-axis for comparable percentages, include isolated readings in the time domain, and display up to five isolated observations explicitly rather than joining missing intervals. Older logs with no `gaugeSamples` show an explicit empty state. Imported logs with malformed optional gauge entries retain their normal hunt, hit, and DPS history: unrecognizable gauge samples and nested elements are skipped, while a recognizable monster observation with an invalid timestamp is retained only as a non-plottable gap marker (`t=-1`) so neither viewer connects across a known missing/clock-corrected interval. Current scope is trend visualization only; future inferred/verified event timelines, tenderize/enrage/sever gauges, and deeper coverage/authority metadata remain separate in #18/#19 and live validation #17. This viewer code can be reviewed and tested without running MHW, but must not be taken as runtime gauge verification.

## Stored-sample quality reporting (issue #19; next stacked slice)

Both SolidJS and GPUI viewers report **counts of stored observations per selected monster and metric**, not estimated gameplay coverage. Observation-source changes are counted in stored capture order, even when a source switch occurs on an invalid or out-of-order timestamp; only time-gap lengths require comparable timestamps. The per-monster summary remains visible when all retained samples are non-plottable and no metric is selectable: it reports stored observations, unreadable timestamps, observed inter-capture gaps longer than six seconds, source changes, recorded clock-boundary flags (including the legacy `clockAdjusted` transition fallback), and non-default source labels. Only when a numeric metric is selected do the viewers additionally report usable samples, missing/invalid metric values, and metric-specific out-of-order readings. Non-default source labels are counted but **never promoted into verified host authority**. Invalid timestamps can be retained as non-plottable gap markers; some completely malformed records were skipped during import and cannot be counted. A zero count is therefore **not** proof that no in-game failure occurred.

Both viewers additionally display up to five recent **stored capture-quality interruption markers** per selected monster in capture order: unusable timestamps, observed source-label switches, explicit/inferred clock corrections, backward timestamps and >6-second comparable capture gaps. Markers are *logging evidence*, not gameplay events. Multiple flags on one observation (e.g., an explicit clock correction with a backwards valid timestamp) intentionally yield separate clock-boundary and backwards-time markers; a single record can therefore occupy multiple of the five display slots. The latest-five display is in reverse emitted/capture order, and neither marker establishes an elapsed-time gap across that correction. A missing timestamp is labeled unavailable; elapsed intervals are shown only for comparable stored timestamps. No exact status activation/expiration, proc, break, hit, hunter identity, or match to the hit/DPS clock is inferred. Marker listing works even with no usable numeric gauge and does not allocate global drop totals to a monster.

Top-level `gaugeSamplesDropped` is a **hunt-wide** count of observation intervals omitted at the recording cap, not a per-monster or per-metric count, and must not be allocated to a selected monster. `gaugeClockDiscontinuities` is likewise hunt-wide. Both viewers show these counts independently of monster and metric availability, including when all optional gauge records are rejected during import. There is no reliable denominator for expected in-game observation coverage in schema 3; the UI deliberately shows **no percent coverage, inferred exact event time, or claim of verified reading quality**. Reported gaps require consecutive comparable valid-time *stored* samples for a monster: the comparison resets at a real or inferred clock boundary, an invalid timestamp, or a backward timestamp (which cannot become the next baseline). Source changes are still counted separately in capture order. These counts cannot detect unrecorded failures without additional evidence.

## F9 experimental overlay presentation (issue #20, source-only)

The **experimental monster-gauge capture toggle remains off by default** and requires confirmed MHW build 421810; the overlay presentation setting cannot enable or increase native memory polling. When the experimental capture gate is deliberately enabled, the in-game overlay defaults to a **compact, bounded summary** of up to two locally sampled monsters (name, sampled stamina if available, and counts of observed status/part meter slots). Unknown stamina is explicitly unavailable, never shown as zero. Additional monsters are acknowledged rather than silently omitted.

The F9 `Detailed sampled gauge rows` checkbox is displayed only while the experimental toggle is on and persists independently as `monsterGaugeOverlayDetails` in the existing `settings.json`. Detailed rows reuse the same cached gauge snapshot, with at most three sampled ailment buildup/proc rows and two sampled normal-part flinch-cycle rows per visible monster. A positive **sampled proc counter remains visible even when the current buildup and duration are both zero**; such counter-only statuses are prioritized before active buildup percentage under the three-status cap, with stable ID tie-breaking. This is evidence of an observed counter value, not a proc event timestamp or proof of an authoritative host state. The existing DPS/party and time-trial sections remain separate; no extra native reads, capture cadence changes, build/authority upgrades or verified gameplay events are introduced. All readings stay prominently labeled **local observation only, NOT verified host state**. The pure presentation helper and real file-based settings roundtrip have source-only regression coverage, but manual Windows/Proton legibility, overlays and FPS evidence remain blocked by #17.

### F9 sampled gauge category visibility (source-only follow-up, #20)

While the experimental 421810 gauge preview is **explicitly enabled**, F9 provides three independent switches for **sampled stamina**, **sampled ailments**, and **sampled normal parts**. Each defaults on for existing and fresh settings files and persists in `settings.json` as `monsterGaugeShowStamina`, `monsterGaugeShowAilments`, and `monsterGaugeShowParts`. Both the compact heading and optional detailed rows show only the selected categories; unselected categories are omitted, never displayed as zero. When all categories are disabled, the overlay clearly states `no sampled gauge categories selected`, retaining the bounded monster list and overflow notice.

This is **presentation only** over the same already-cached observations: it cannot change the disabled-by-default experimental capture gate, polling cadence, source authority or log recording. This slice does not introduce an HP gauge, separate sever/tenderize/enrage values, active-target identity selection or a verified in-game event. Regression checks cover each category in compact/detailed modes, the all-disabled fallback, missing observations, display caps and true file-backed legacy/default/persisted settings behavior. Windows/Proton manual F9 behavior, legibility and frame-time measurements remain deferred to #17.

### Manual current-monster overlay selection (source-only #20 follow-up)

When more than one **current cached** monster observation exists and the experimental preview is enabled, F9 offers a bounded **manual sampled-monster selector**. Automatic mode (default) keeps the existing first-two-monster compact/detailed display and overflow notice. Choosing one sampled monster shows only its cached rows, without changing native reads or capture. The selector uses the sampled entity address **and** monster name to distinguish same-name entries, and clears on a missing roster, despawn, changed poller generation, disabled capture or the next hunt. It is deliberately **not persisted**: an MHW entity pointer is not a stable cross-hunt ID, and this UI does **not** claim an active game target, monster lock-on, authoritative host identity or precise event attribution. The F9 choice list is capped at eight sampled entries with an overflow note.

F9 buttons are keyed by **sampled pointer + name + poller generation**, not roster row index, so a roster reorder between pointer-down and pointer-up cannot reassign a button to a different sampled monster. Same-name sampled monsters receive distinct, **nonpersistent** `sample #1`/`sample #2` labels sorted by current pointer identity; the currently selected row is visibly marked. Dear ImGui's `###pick` ID suffix keeps each button's **full widget identity** unchanged when its visible text changes from `Show` to `Selected` (unlike `##pick`, which includes visible text in the ID). Tests cover the actual label passed to the widget and the composed scoped identity. Exact duplicate records share one UI choice instead of colliding ImGui IDs. The pointer-derived control IDs are internal and never exposed as stable monster identities.

The production poller exposes a presentation-only generation counter invalidated on detected roster/identity/large-HP-reset changes. The existing same-pointer reuse limitations still require live-game verification in #17. Tests exercise manual/automatic modes, same-name entities, reordering, despawn, invalid selections, generation reset and disabled capture without changing poll cadence. Manual F9 usability and frame-time impact are still unverified until #17.

### Explicit gauge availability and source provenance (source-only #20 follow-up)

F9 now explains the experimental gauge state using the **existing capture switch, confirmed 421810/map gate, in-quest state and current cached monster count**, distinguishing `off`, `unsupported/unconfirmed build`, `outside active quest`, `enabled but no current samples`, and `local-unverified samples`. The existing diagnostics line uses the same pure classification so it cannot falsely imply a guest/host-authoritative reading. A capture-off click immediately suppresses any cached gauge values in the HUD, even before the next 100 ms poll clears them. Any F9 capture-toggle transition also resets the production poller, the cached gauge array, and the transient manual monster selection immediately; turning capture back on cannot briefly resurrect stale pre-toggle readings. The menu's same-frame availability message also treats existing pre-toggle samples as unavailable. When capture is enabled, supported, and in quest but no samples exist, a short no-current-samples hint appears under the party table instead of an unexplained empty section. Default-off HUD remains unchanged and uncluttered.

**No current sample** is **not** a measured zero, and **local-unverified** is not confirmed multiplayer host authority. This slice does not assert why samples are absent or that any cached samples have authoritative freshness; time-based stale-age diagnosis, actual build offsets and pointer reuse still require #17 validation. The F9 status is recomputed after an experimental-toggle click for same-frame feedback. The provenance model performs no extra native reads, modifies no poll/recording cadence, does not promote any unsupported gauges, and never displays cached values while the experimental or build/quest gate is closed. Production-model regression covers each state, precedence, no-data HUD policy, immediate toggle recomputation and source labels.

### Time-bounded cached sample visibility (source-only #20 follow-up)

A cached gauge snapshot is now timestamped only after a **successful non-empty native gauge sample**, using the same process-monotonic clock already supplied to the production poller. F9 diagnostics and the HUD use this sampled age when classifying experimental gauge availability. With capture enabled on the supported build and an active quest, observations older than **2.5 seconds**, or with a missing/invalid age, are classified as **stale** and hidden from the HUD/manual selector until another successful normal-cadence sample arrives. The HUD shows a concise stale-sample hint rather than a value that appears live. No data and stale cached data are distinct; neither is a measured zero. The original **local-unverified** label remains, and a recently sampled reading is **not** proof of host authority or correct offsets.

This only constrains how long a cached reading may appear current when update callbacks stall; it does not change the native **one-second gauge read** / **100-ms party poll** cadence, add reader calls, timestamp game events, or claim gameplay-level freshness. The poller's sample timestamp is cleared on cache reset, generation replacement, failed read, empty capture, and experimental disable. Source-only regression exercises successful/skip/reacquire/empty/disable paths, a stalled render, exact age boundary, unknown/negative/non-finite age, all gate precedence, and missing-vs-stale presentation. **Age-only staleness is strictly a display mask:** both HUD and F9 hide stale values/choices without clearing an existing manual monster selection. A fresh same-generation roster validates and restores that choice, including a selected third monster; a missing selected monster on the next fresh roster, a changed poller generation even while stale, capture disabled, or a truly empty sample still clears it. The same production availability-aware selection method is used in HUD, F9 and regression tests. Real frame ordering, brief hitch tolerance, UI legibility and source/authority still require #17.

### F9 same-frame verbose diagnostics consistency (source-only #20 follow-up)

### Bounded gauge display labels without changing sampled identity (source-only #20 follow-up)

The experimental HUD and F9 monster selector now limit **display-only labels** to 32 Unicode grapheme clusters, with an ellipsis for longer names. Tabs, newlines and repeated whitespace become a single visual space, and **each normalized space counts toward the 32-grapheme budget**; blank names receive a neutral fallback. Long ailment and part names in expanded HUD rows are similarly bounded. This reduces the chance that unusual labels expand the auto-sized overlay, but does not substitute for in-game responsive layout testing.

The **full original monster name and instance** remain unchanged for manual selection, generation detection, saved observations and stable Dear ImGui widget IDs. Full names that shorten to the same displayed F9 label receive deterministic markers ordered by **complete (instance, full name) identity**, even when two different sampled names temporarily share the same instance. Source regression checks same-pointer/full-name collisions, ordering and selection, normalized-space label budgets, full-identity invalidation, long/blank/whitespace names, combining accents, emoji, and expanded rows. No new native reads, polling, HP/DPS, recording, settings or source-authority claims. Actual Windows/Proton rendering and frame-time remain gated by #17.

## Observed counter-change intervals (next viewer slice, issue #19)

Both viewers now show a single **combined, most-recent-five counter-change timeline** for the selected monster across *all* recorded ailment proc and normal-part completed-flinch counters, independently of the selected percentage metric or even the existence of a chartable numeric percentage. The rows are ordered by the end of each stored observation interval, then start, with a stable metric key for ties. Each row identifies the type of sampled counter rather than claiming an event. This supersedes the former selected-metric-only interval list. Both viewers use the existing per-metric counter-interval validator and the same source/clock/time restrictions. When counter-only imported status observations exist, they can be reported without inventing a buildup/duration reading. This is a bounded latest-five presentation, not exhaustive event telemetry.

Both viewers can list increases in **recorded** ailment proc counters and normal-part completed-flinch counters, alongside the selected percentage history. Each entry means only that the counter increased **between two consecutive comparable local samples** (shown as a start/end interval), not that a specific proc, break, hit or attacker occurred at either endpoint. A single interval can contain several increments.

Optional imported gauge IDs and counters are limited to **signed 32-bit integers** (−2,147,483,648 through 2,147,483,647) in both SolidJS and GPUI. Out-of-range, mathematically fractional, string or otherwise malformed counters remain unknown, never silently wrapped or interpreted as zero; invalid IDs are excluded and valid neighbouring observations remain available. Imported JSON numeric spellings such as `1.0` and `2e0` are accepted by both viewers **only when mathematically integral and within signed i32 bounds**, matching JavaScript JSON parsing; GPUI does not round fractional counters. An optional part flinch counter or next-break threshold may be null even when that part's remaining/max gauge reading is valid. Nonnegative counter eligibility applies only after signed-integer import validation; negative but in-range counters are not treated as observations. Both viewers use the same malformed imported hunt fixture to check these bounds, integral-float JSON spellings, genuine fractions, duplicate part-ID counter selection, and resulting intervals.

For imported samples with duplicate **ailment or part IDs**, the per-metric proc/flinch comparison selects the first *valid nonnegative integer counter* among entries with that ID. Earlier duration-only ailment entries or duplicate part records with missing, negative or invalid flinch counters must not hide a later real counter. The counter is never inferred from duration or buildup and no fields are combined across entries. Comparisons reset at missing/invalid observations, clock discontinuities, changes of observation source, non-monotonic or >6-second gaps, missing counters and counter decreases. No event is synthesized across such gaps and no counter is inferred as zero merely because an imported optional field is absent. This is retrospective analysis of `local-unverified` samples, not new memory access, an authoritative in-game event feed or synchronized HP/DPS attribution. Exact proc/break events, cross-client source authority and gameplay validation remain deferred under #17/#18.

## Explicitly deferred

- **Still experimental:** monster-generation inference and cumulative HP-damage banking from identity/large HP recovery are conservative. Difficulty scaling without a stable hit-by-hit source can hide a hit coincident with the exact rebase tick; a complete monster enumeration that observes a pointer disappearing clears only its *live* monster reference; the historical mN is retained and any same-pointer reappearance receives a new mN even with identical type/name/variant/HP. Torn/failed enumerations never count as disappearance, and successful enumeration with an individually unreadable monster HP/type retains that pointer using the tracker's known live-instance list. Pointer reuse that occurs **without an observed absence or identity/HP signal** may remain ambiguous until runtime testing. The normalized gauge clock is explicitly separate from raw hit/quest timestamps after a discontinuity.
- **Live verification remains required:** `0`/`1`-terminated ailment-list packing, structure owners and offsets, native-copy overhead under Windows/Proton and host/guest authority are still unverified.
- Separate severable part memory region (`+0x1FC8`), tenderization-duration array (`+0x1C458`), enrage state, exact proc timing, per-hit buildup, host/guest authority detection, polished widgets, and **verified proc/break event charts and event timelines**. Both viewers now provide **read-only sampled gauge-history graphs** in stacked PR #36; neither viewer provides precise proc/break timing or verified combat-event attribution.
- Current collision hit damage continues to mean **HP damage by contact part**; it is not part buildup, KO, or ailment damage.
- No game-memory writes, remote service, or new detours.
